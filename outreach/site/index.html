Why we're doing it

It might not be good enough yet. We'd still like the number.

Other groups have done individual steps well. We haven't found anyone who ran the whole chain.

Every step is improving quickly. At some point the chain crosses a threshold. Without a starting point we won't know when.

So we start small. Twelve compounds, two targets, a handful of quantum labels. Explore the parameter space properly at that size, then scale only what survives. Four tiers, and each gate has to be green before we move up. The hackathon sits at tier two.

The testbed

Most drugs fail on selectivity, not potency.

A compound designed for one protein hits its close relatives too, and the programme dies late and expensively. Conventional screening can't separate them: it flattens a three-dimensional pocket into a fingerprint and throws away exactly what makes two pockets different — local geometry, induced fit, electrostatics, the way both protein and ligand move.

Our driving case is CDK9 against CDK7: two kinases similar enough that telling them apart is the whole problem.

We're building a staged pipeline where fast AI models do the volume work and GPU quantum chemistry is spent only where it changes the answer. Four times in that chain a learned surrogate stands in for something expensive — and every one has to declare where it may be trusted.

The chain

Eight steps. Each cheap enough to run on everything reaching it, each allowed to be wrong in a characterised way that the next step corrects. Four of them hand an expensive calculation to a learned model.

It comes down to one residue

Twenty residues line the CDK9 site. Two of them are positions where CDK9 differs from all three counter-targets, and one of those sits at the hinge, 3.2 Å from the ligand.

CDK9 carries a cysteine where the other three carry methionine. A 2D fingerprint has nothing to work with here — the compound is the same in every case. The difference is one atom in the protein.

Every stage works at a different scale

Same structure, same camera, only the radius changes. Between the smallest region and the largest crop, the quantum cost moves by more than two orders of magnitude — and nobody has told us where the accuracy stops improving. So we sweep it.

Questions

Do I need to know anything about drug discovery?

No. Several roles need the opposite — someone who will ask why we're doing it that way.

Do I need to be able to code?

No. Pose triage and benchmark critique involve none. The "own a configuration" path is one command, with someone beside you.

Two days is a lot. Is there a smaller version?

Three levels: full participant, 90-minute clinic on day 1, or reviewer — read the plan, tell us what's wrong with it, come to the close. The third costs an hour and is how several of the most useful contributions arrive.

What if it doesn't work?

Then we know in two days rather than two years. Six gates were written before any data existed. A clean "no" is a successful outcome.

What hardware?

Dual NVIDIA GB10 boxes, ours for the month beforehand and during the event, plus HGX H200 for benchmarks. Your first run won't queue behind someone's sweep.

Why are you on different days to the main event?

Those are the two days we have. The global event runs 21-22 October and registration is open to anyone, so sign up for that too. Wednesday the 21st is open if you want to keep going, and it's the global event's opening day.
