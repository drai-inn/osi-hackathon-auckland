The problem

Most drugs fail on selectivity, not potency.

A compound designed for one protein hits its close relatives too, and the programme dies late and expensively. Conventional screening can't separate them: it flattens a three-dimensional pocket into a fingerprint and throws away exactly what makes two pockets different — local geometry, induced fit, electrostatics, the way both protein and ligand move.

Our driving case is CDK9 against CDK7: two kinases similar enough that telling them apart is the whole problem.

The question underneath

When does what you learn from a small experiment actually predict the large one?

That question belongs to ecology with one field season, clinical work with small cohorts, materials with slow synthesis. Every one of them has a pipeline of configurable choices, an expensive evaluation and no ability to grid-search. We happen to have a convenient testbed — which is why this is a cross-disciplinary event rather than a computational chemistry one.

New to AI for science?

Good. Within the first hour you'll own one configuration of the pipeline — one command, about twenty minutes, and your run appears on the shared dashboard as a data point in the final analysis. Not a tutorial. The actual experiment, which needs many configurations and is better with a room of people each running one. Bring a laptop; that's all.

Questions

Do I need to know anything about drug discovery?

No. Several roles need the opposite — someone who will ask why we're doing it that way.

Do I need to be able to code?

No. Pose triage and benchmark critique involve none. The "own a configuration" path is one command, with someone beside you.

Two days is a lot. Is there a smaller version?

Three levels: full participant, 90-minute clinic on day 1, or reviewer — read the plan, tell us what's wrong with it, come to the close. The third costs an hour and is how several of the most useful contributions arrive.

What if it doesn't work?

Then we know in two days rather than two years. Six gates were written before any data existed. A clean "no" is a successful outcome.

What hardware?

Dual NVIDIA GB10 boxes, ours for the month beforehand and during the event, plus HGX H200 for benchmarks. Your first run won't queue behind someone's sweep.

Why are you two days before the main event?

Local scheduling — we can't run past the 21st. It turns out to be an advantage: we finish sixteen hours before the earliest hub in the world opens, so what we build is available to everyone else from their first minute. If you want more, Wednesday the 21st is the global event's opening day and you can carry the work straight in.
